**PHANTOM: project review, from research idea to current implementation**

Review date: September 5, 2026. Code reviewed: `f64aea8` on `main`. This review adds documentation only.

**Updated after read-only SSH inspection of compute3.** The [remote evidence supplement](project_review_20260905_remote.md) and [117-episode ledger](review_20260905/episodes.csv) add actual checkpoint/log inspection and recomputed evaluation results. They confirm modest teacher learning and reduced joint-speed spikes, correct the close-height comparison below, and identify a current busy-server/duplicate-launch failure. The original repository-only assessment is amended where the new evidence changes it.

**My assessment: the project is stuck because its experimental feedback loop has not kept up with its implementation.** The team has repeatedly changed the model, input representation, execution rules, stopping conditions and measurement tools together. Consequently, a failed trial often cannot distinguish a weak policy from an execution defect, an invalid starting state or an inappropriate endpoint. The latest evidence suggests progress from acquisition failures to retention/carry failures, but the project has not converted that progress into a reproducible task-success result or a test of tactile distillation.

There is substantial useful engineering here. I would preserve the data system, teacher checkpoints, checkpoint/config safeguards, replay tools and recording infrastructure. I would narrow the active research question and make the next session identify one cause. A larger model, another broad fine-tune bundle, or another general audit does not presently have the strongest justification.

**1. The idea is coherent, but the original proposal bundles several unproven claims.**

The strongest version of the idea is: tactile sensing supplies privileged training information about contact; a policy retaining vision, proprioception and the permitted wrist signal can learn useful contact representations from it and perform better than an otherwise comparable policy without that training. That is a meaningful empirical question.

The proposal additionally asks the same project to establish a better heterogeneous tactile representation (HHT), anticipatory routing (ACC), structured asymmetric generation (ACE), uncertainty-based control, haptic trajectory distillation (HID), on-policy correction, force-safety fine-tuning (HID-S) and occlusion robustness. Each adds both implementation dependencies and a required control experiment. The original matrix is roughly 2,100 main trials plus 960 ablation trials, while later planning assumes one rig and 15–20 rig-hours. The current four data task names also differ from the five proposed tasks. [Proposal](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/pipeline.md#L343), [original review constraints](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/docs/review_20260828/REVIEW_SYNTHESIS.md#L5).

The architecture has legitimate precedent: Cosmos Policy generates action/state/value modalities through latent-frame injection; Dream-Tac jointly models visual, tactile and action outputs with contact-aware fusion. Their existence supports the architectural direction, not the equivalence of PHANTOM's training recipe, hardware or outcomes. PHANTOM's potential contribution is the measured benefit of its particular contact representation and transfer mechanism. [Cosmos Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16163), [Dream-Tac](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08737v1).

Three assumptions need narrower wording:

- Distillation can transfer useful regularities, but it cannot make an unobserved physical state identifiable from inputs that contain no information about it. Contact prediction must be assessed under the student's actual observability, particularly under occlusion.
- The original story relies on an informative wrist force signal. The rig YAML explicitly says the real arm is CB3, its wrist wrench is a current-based estimate, and `generation: e-series` is declared to bypass a schema restriction. This should be represented honestly in configuration and claims; it is not evidence that the assumed precise wrist sensor is available. [Hardware declaration](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/configs/hardware.nuc.yaml#L9).
- The deployed tactile stream is around 8 Hz, while contact targets use a one-second latent grid and the gate lookahead is 0.5 seconds. That supports a test of coarse contact anticipation; it does not yet support the proposal's broad account of resolving tens-of-milliseconds transients at the sensor's advertised 120 Hz. [Actual rates](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/configs/hardware.nuc.yaml#L65), [window construction](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/data/windows.py#L137).

**4. Why I think you are stuck, in priority order.**

**A. You are debugging the learned policy and its controller simultaneously.** The effective system is the neural policy plus sampling, history construction, chunk execution, the uncertainty governor, veto/recovery, grip latch and stop rules. Recent improvements change several of these. More successful acquisition can therefore produce more carry-phase guard stops and appear to regress under an undifferentiated success label. The project needs separate acquisition, hold, transport and completion outcomes.

There is a current execution-contract defect: the executor updates `_last_cmd` before `servo_l`; the driver's default-on IK guard may then silently hold. Subsequent plans can be anchored to a command that never reached the arm. I reproduced the driver's silent-return behavior with fake RTDE. The opt-in limiter has the same disagreement when shortening a command. This does not prove a particular historical drift magnitude, but it prevents treating commanded and streamed pose as equivalent. [Executor](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/deploy/executor.py#L398), [driver](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/drivers/real/ur.py#L428).

**B. The trials do not yet close the scientific feedback loop.** The current code still has no single authoritative experiment artifact combining all effective controls, the loaded model identity and structured trial pairing. Recent lift/latch overrides are omitted from `deploy_overrides`; the server reports a checkpoint path rather than its source revision and weight digest, while the episode git tag comes from the client. An old resident server can survive a client update. [Overrides and tags](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/scripts/run_deploy.py#L735), [server information](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/inference/remote.py#L148).

The evaluation package has deeper defects than documentation drift. I ran actual current functions on synthetic episodes with identical physical events and different time origins:

| Clock offset | Returned anticipation lead | Event F1 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 s | [0.5, 0.5] s | 1.0 |
| 1,234 s | [1,234.5, 1,234.5] s | 0.0 |

One physical onset is counted for each finger, and host-clock planner timestamps are compared directly with master-clock sensor timestamps. A separate probe of 0/16 successes returned CI [0, 0]. The outcome has uncertainty even when all observed trials fail. These are current, reproduced defects in [metrics](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/eval/metrics.py#L75) and [aggregation](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/eval/aggregate.py#L32).

The campaign runner also groups trials by system, never passes its ledger seed into episode execution, and lacks the main launcher's current inference options. Thus the manual rig procedure and the advertised evaluation campaign are materially different paths. Fix or explicitly retire that path before using its tables. [Campaign runner](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/eval/trial_runner.py#L64), [policy construction](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/scripts/run_eval.py#L56).

Actual September logs expose another current prerequisite: the resident server's single-client loop blocks identity probes while busy. PICK treats the timeout as no matching server and enables local fallback. A recorded September 4 fallback fails three RTDE ownership checks before creating an episode. Separate busy from absent and acquire cross-process rig ownership before control/preflight actions. The associated historical EOF crash is already fixed. The [remote supplement](project_review_20260905_remote.md) joins source behavior to the specific log timeline without assuming the identity of the occupied controller.

**C. Much of the added data did not contain the missing behavior.** The recovery protocol requested successful corrections from wrong pre-grasp states. The intake code explicitly says QC found the August 22 positive starts inside the existing demonstration distribution and removed the `recovery` tag. The undergrasp negatives continue with an empty gripper. Correctly suppressing their action loss teaches the contact heads about failure, but supplies no successful reopen/reposition/reclose action. More examples of ordinary success are not equivalent to expert corrections at encountered failure states. [Requested protocol](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/docs/recovery_demos_protocol.md#L3), [what intake actually found](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/tools/intake_recovery.py#L12).

**D. The student deployment path does not yet test the headline claim.** Its neural input path removes tactile, but the runtime still starts tactile workers; safety publishes pad loads; the default grip latch uses those loads; and the recommended veto reads pads to trigger or suppress recovery. Those signals change task actions. A successful run under this controller would demonstrate a neural student without tactile inputs under tactile-assisted control. It would not establish that the complete robot system works without fingertip sensing. [Workers](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/deploy/runtime.py#L184), [latch](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/deploy/executor.py#L433), [recovery](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/deploy/planner.py#L493).

Keep emergency protection and evaluation instruments explicit. A tactile emergency stop is also privileged system assistance and must be disclosed and counted. The experiment must distinguish sensors used only to score outcomes from sensors whose readings alter task execution; simply masking neural inputs is insufficient.

**E. Implementation completeness has repeatedly been mistaken for research readiness.** The initial pipeline arrived at once, and `docs/STATUS.md` still says the code is complete and that remaining work is hardware or running it. Later history disproves that assessment. There are many valuable regression tests, but real contracts were discovered after deployment: single-open sensors, actual gripper observations, causal action labels, live gradient synchronization and actuator acceptance. Mock tests help when they enforce those contracts; a broad passing count does not prove the experiment is meaningful. [Stale status](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/docs/STATUS.md#L17).

The unusually candid retractions are a strength. The operational weakness is that corrected conclusions, active commands and planned claims remain scattered across append-only documents, temporary remote results and box-local copies. Future work needs a decision attached to a frozen measurement, with one owner for the experimental readout.

**6. Concrete next steps, with decisions attached.**

**First, spend the next working day establishing the experiment, not extending its architecture.** Write one current manifest for one waffles task: exact final outcome; stage labels; allowed inputs; checkpoint digest; client/server revisions; complete effective controller settings; data/normalization provenance; seed; realized robot start and object placement. Record every allocated attempt, including stops, manual interventions, aborted recordings and missing labels. A missing hold tail can make a grasp metric inconclusive; a controller stop must not silently disappear from the full-task denominator.

Recover the September 4 labels where synchronized video and sensor records genuinely establish them. Preserve ambiguous outcomes as unknown. Produce an episode-level table separating acquisition, maintained hold, carry completion and reason for termination. This can establish whether the dominant present failure is policy opening, infeasible motion, premature stopping or something else. Stop using an automatic suggestion to label a guard-interrupted grasp as evidence of full task completion.

**Second, resolve whether the deployed controller can execute the task.** Repair command acceptance and stop-state logging. Replay known successful demonstration trajectories through the same deployment representation, timing, limits and gripper handling, first in offline/fake-controller checks and then in supervised hardware trials. This is a diagnostic experiment, not evidence for learned-policy generalization. Direct joint-space teleoperation replay alone is insufficient if policy deployment uses delta-TCP/IK execution.

Use approximately ten resets as an engineering check, with task completion and maximum tracking error tolerances fixed from the measured clearances beforehand. If known-good trajectories fail under the runtime, prioritize controller geometry/timing/phase semantics. If those pass but teacher proposals leave the reachable envelope, investigate policy-state coverage and carry behavior. Any runtime change starts a new evaluation block. RTC is relevant if measured delay remains limiting, but a known freeze-and-inpaint method should be evaluated against the actual latency budget rather than added speculatively. [RTC primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339).

**Third, run one frozen, paired diagnostic comparison.** Fix one controller and compare v5_6 with ftA_1500 using matched placements, robot starts and sampling seeds, alternating which model goes first. Begin with 16–20 pairs in the demonstrated placement range. This is a pilot for large behavioral differences, not a guarantee of statistical significance or a paper-sized sample. Choose the primary outcome before observing the results; report absolute task-success difference, stage outcomes, interventions, timing and uncertainty. Separate inference seeds from independent training runs. Use trial/session/placement as appropriate statistical units, never denoising windows or replans as independent robot successes.

If testing a controller intervention instead, hold the checkpoint fixed and change that intervention alone. Keep equipment protections intact; use offline/shadow comparisons where removing an intervention would make a physical comparison inappropriate. Do not again compare a plain checkpoint against a different checkpoint plus NFE, K-seed, history and veto changes and attribute the result to one ingredient.

**Fourth, collect the behavior that the failure analysis actually calls for.** From representative failed pre-grasp, empty-close and near-boundary carry states, record successful expert correction segments, with a safe handover and explicit provenance. Validate that starting states match policy failures. Hold out later sessions/placements. A small verified correction set is more informative than another large batch labeled recovery solely because that was the collection intention. The reason for using on-policy states is the sequential distribution shift addressed by DAgger; it does not imply that an unreliable teacher is automatically a reliable correction oracle. [DAgger](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html).

**Fifth, test the research claim with the smallest adequate controls.** Once the execution baseline is stable, compare the tactile teacher, HID student and a student-input model trained without distillation. The no-privilege control should share the same public pretrained backbone, remaining inputs and action demonstrations, but must not inherit tactile-teacher weights or tactile auxiliary supervision. A teacher-initialized student without HID losses can be an additional control for HID's incremental benefit; it cannot be the sole control for privileged tactile training. Match data split, checkpoint selection, runtime assistance and a declared training budget. Include a simpler action-only policy to test whether the full video/contact objective earns its cost. Tactile-null teacher inference remains a useful sensitivity test, but cannot substitute for these trained controls.

The existing `train_teacher --student` arm is specifically a **no teacher-distillation** control, not a no-privileged-tactile-training control: student windows still construct ground-truth tactile contact/event targets, and the training loss still supervises them. It is useful for isolating the added value of teacher distillation over direct auxiliary supervision. A claim about the benefit of privileged tactile training also needs the separate action-only/no-tactile-target control described above. [CLI semantics](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/train/train_teacher.py#L310), [student targets](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/data/windows.py#L311), [shared losses](https://github.com/Advanced-Robotic-Manipulation/phantom/blob/f64aea89e2ec48c6975a8806476c29170beac189/phantom/model/rf.py#L419).
