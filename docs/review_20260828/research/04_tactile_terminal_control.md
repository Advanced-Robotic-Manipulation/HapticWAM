# Visuotactile terminal-phase control and contact-gated action heads — literature review

## 1. Papers surveyed

## 2. How the literature makes the action head actually *use* the contact signal

This is the crux of our bug (ACC gate correctly says p_none≈0.99, but the action head lifts anyway). Six distinct mechanisms recur, in increasing order of how forcefully they compel use:

1. **Put contact/force in the action itself, not just the observation** (Just Add Force, TacDiffusion). If the loss requires predicting force/wrench jointly with pose, the network cannot get a free pass on ignoring it — the training signal *is* the contact channel. This is architecturally the cheapest change and directly attacks "gate exists as an aux head but the action head never has to be consistent with it."
2. **Route information conditionally through attention, gated by a learned or thresholded contact probability** (FeelWorld's `z_o += g_t · CrossAttn(...)`). This is closest to what a "gate" should mean: pre-contact, tactile is architecturally *suppressed* (matches physical reality — nothing to feel yet, so don't let noise leak in); once gate fires, it is *mandatory* input to the visual/action stream. Crucially the gate threshold is also used **downstream in planning** (two-stage CEM: pure visual-goal cost pre-contact, tactile+slip cost only post-contact) — the same signal is a routing switch in two different subsystems, not decoration.
3. **Decode-time concatenation, not encode-time conditioning** (RDP's asymmetric tokenizer). RDP explicitly separates "high-level strategy" (slow, tactile-blind) from "precise realization" (fast decoder, tactile-*required* input to reconstruct the action). This maps well onto our chunk/replan split: the flow-matching sampler decides an abstract chunk every 0.9 s; a fast tactile-conditioned decoder could be the one thing allowed to touch cpk/gate at 10 Hz within the chunk.
4. **Modality dropout as a training-time forcing function** (match-lighting paper). Randomly zeroing tactile 50% of the time doesn't add new information — it prevents the network from building a shortcut where vision+proprio alone already predict the (easy, unimodal) training trajectories well enough that the gate/cpk gradient is negligible. Their ablation shows a 2× improvement even at *test-time vision-only* inference from dropout during training alone — direct evidence that dropout regularizes toward using the modality when present rather than any change to inference-time architecture.
5. **Move the response out of the learned head entirely and into a scripted override** (Disambiguate Gripper State). This is the "give up on the network" move: don't rely on gradient descent to have wired p(empty-close) into the policy; hard-code the rule "if pseudo-tactile==empty-close, force reopen" downstream of the network. It gets 100% disturbance robustness vs 20–70% for the *learned* alternative, cheaply. This is directly applicable and low-risk for us with 3 weeks left.
6. **Make a downstream RL/residual objective's reward literally depend on using tactile correctly** (MimicTouch, ViTaL, TACO). Instead of hoping a BC loss creates the right gradient, train a small residual/correction module whose only way to get reward (or to reduce recognize-imagine-label loss) is to condition on contact state. TACO's "knowledge-insulated" framing is notable: it isolates the tactile-adaptation gradient from the base VLA's weights so tactile correction doesn't get diluted by the (much larger) vision-language loss surface — an analogue of our LoRA r16 adapter potentially being too small/shared to carry a strong contact-conditioning signal against the dominant video-prediction loss.

## 3. A concrete gate-driven terminal controller for PHANTOM

Given: ACC gate p(contact soon) + event classes, cpk (CoP, wrench, contact events) fed back as prev_cpk, 16-step/10 Hz chunk (1.6 s), NFE=5 flow matching with persistent noise, 0.9 s replan, and the observed failure (decelerate/stop 65–120 mm high, close on air with gate correctly at p_none 0.99, then lift).

**Design — scripted layer wrapping the learned sampler, no retraining required first:**
- **Close-mask gate (mechanism #5, Disambiguate-Gripper-style):** at inference, mask/veto any commanded gripper-close transition in the executed chunk unless `p(contact soon) > θ_close` (start θ_close≈0.5, tune against the 08-20 successful-session gate trace) **or** `z ≤ z_floor_task` (a hard fallback so a genuinely low-confidence-but-correct close near the table isn't blocked forever). This directly stops "close at 90 mm because that's what a demo-length timer says" independent of whether the action head itself ever learns to condition on the gate.
- **Phantom-grasp replan override (mechanism #5 + FeelWorld's two-stage idea):** at each 0.9 s replan boundary, if `prev_cpk.event == none/miss` **and** the previous chunk commanded close, classify as phantom-grasp and **do not** sample the next chunk normally. Instead splice in a scripted recovery: reopen to demo-aperture, step down a fixed Δz (e.g. −20 mm, capped by z_floor/STOP hitbox), and re-run the sampler from that corrected state with reduced noise persistence (break out of the "slow mode" attractor implicated in the 33 vs 45–50 mm/s descent-speed multimodality).
- **Two-phase guidance schedule (mechanism #2/#3, FeelWorld + RDP):** split the chunk conceptually into *approach* (low guidance weight 1.0 as now, tactile heads free-running but not gating) and *terminal* (once `p(contact soon)` crosses a lower pre-alert threshold θ_alert < θ_close, e.g. 0.2–0.3, bump CFG guidance and reduce noise persistence for just the terminal sub-chunk) so the commit decision is sampled from a *sharper*, less multi-modal distribution exactly where multimodality is costliest.
- **Contact-anticipation seed selection (novel, not seen in the fetched literature — see §5):** we already sample with NFE=5 flow matching; at each replan, sample K≥3 noise seeds, roll each forward through the model's *own* predicted p(contact) trajectory for the coming chunk, and select the seed whose predicted-contact curve best matches a canonical "monotonically rising to committed-close near expected grasp height" shape (mined from the 08-20 successful session and the demo distribution). This uses the *existing* learned ACC output as a scorer to collapse multi-modality, rather than adding new architecture.
- **Learned addition, second pass (mechanism #1, cheap):** add a consistency loss between the action head's gripper-close logit at each future step and the ACC gate's p(contact) at the same step (`L_consistency = BCE(close_logit, gate_target)` or a penalty when the model predicts close while gate predicts p_none high) — the FeelWorld/Just-Add-Force move of making contact part of what the action loss is graded on, so over more fine-tune steps the *unscripted* policy itself narrows toward gate-consistent behavior, reducing reliance on the scripted veto.

**What stays learned vs scripted:**
- *Learned:* ACC gate & event classes, cpk (CoP/wrench/contact events), the flow-matching action distribution, the seed-scoring function (reuses ACC's own contact prediction).
- *Scripted:* close-mask threshold logic, phantom-grasp recovery macro (reopen/backoff/redescend), replan-cadence override rule, z_floor/STOP hitbox (already exists in the stack).

**Ablation for the paper** (isolates the causal claim "contact anticipation, not just tactile presence, fixes the terminal commit"):
1. Full stack (gate-driven controller + consistency loss) vs
2. Gate available only as conditioning input, no scripted veto/no consistency loss (≈ current baseline that fails) vs
3. No ACC at all (vision-only action head) vs
4. Scripted veto only, no consistency loss (isolates the "give up on the network" mechanism, mirrors Disambiguate-Gripper-State) vs
5. Consistency loss only, no scripted veto (isolates whether architecture-level forcing alone suffices) vs
6. Seed-selection-by-predicted-contact vs random-seed vs best-of-K-by-CFG-alone (isolates whether the gain is from *using the contact signal* specifically, not just multi-sample averaging).

## 4. Auto-labeling rollout success from tactile for a self-improvement loop

Adopt the 4-phase structure from *Learning Gentle Grasping* (before-grasp → initial-grasp → regrasp → lift), mapped onto PHANTOM's existing signals:

`success = (contact_area_peak(DM-Tac) > A_min) AND (force_peak within [F_min, F_max], i.e. not a crush) AND (Robotiq OBJ == grasped-flag) AND (z_lift_delta over the post-close window > Δz_min) AND (no re-open / no OBJ→no-object flip within the confirmation window)`.

Refinements suggested by the literature:
- Borrow FeelWorld's **focal-loss slip classifier** design (class imbalance ~1%) to catch *delayed* failures — object grasped and lifted but slips out during transport, which our AND-gate above would miss if it only checks a short post-close window.
- Borrow TACO's **Recognize-Imagine-Label** framing directly for the DAgger-lite loop we already have scaffolded: train a lightweight "progress/outcome" classifier on the labeled segments we already possess (280 ordinary successes + 45 under-grasp-fail demos with action-weight-0), use it to auto-label the 50–150 planned rig rollouts, and feed AWR-weighted updates (existing `beta_awr`, `max_weight` config) — this is exactly TACO's "advantage-conditioned" idea, and its ablation (+32 pp specifically attributable to *insulating* the tactile-adaptation gradient from the base model) argues for updating via the AWR/DAgger path against a small adapter rather than full backbone fine-tuning, to avoid diluting the video-prediction loss that already dominates gradient magnitude.
- MimicTouch's data point is the most directly comparable budget precedent: 40%→96% success from ~3 hours of *residual*, tactile-gated correction on top of an already-reasonable offline policy — i.e., a small number of real-robot corrective interactions, not full retraining, produced the terminal-phase fix. This is a strong existence proof that our 50–150-rollout budget is enough *if* the update targets the terminal-commit decision specifically (matching our own AWR scaffolding) rather than the whole trajectory.

## 5. Novelty angle vs. existing tactile-IL papers

Nearly every paper above (RDP, FeelWorld, Just Add Force, TacDiffusion, MimicTouch) **keeps the tactile sensor on the robot at deployment** — tactile is a live control input, not just a training signal. PHANTOM's actual claim is the opposite of that literature's default setup: a **sensor-free student**, distilled from a tactile-anticipation teacher, that must reproduce contact-aware commit behavior from vision+proprio alone at test time. That is a teacher–student / privileged-information distillation framing (structurally close to RMA-style teacher-student in legged locomotion) applied to visuotactile grasping, which is rare in the surveyed set — FeelWorld and TACO are the closest analogues, but both still run their tactile world model/adapter live on the deployed policy rather than distilling it away. The paper's strongest, most defensible claim is therefore not "tactile signals help" (well established, e.g. Just Add Force's own 11.5% vs 20% null-grasp gap already shows *even a tactile-equipped* policy has residual phantom grasps) but "a model that never touches the object at test time can still anticipate contact and avoid the exact premature-commit failure that plagues vision-only BC, because it was shaped during training by a tactile-anticipating teacher, not because it senses anything at runtime." The gate-driven terminal-controller ablation in §3 is what lets reviewers see this mechanistically: it isolates how much of the fix is "any contact signal, scripted or not" (§3 ablations 3–5) versus how much survives once the tactile sensor itself is entirely absent at deployment (the actual student condition) — a distinction none of the surveyed papers test, since none of them ask their tactile-conditioned policy to work with the tactile channel *removed at test time by design*.

A secondary, smaller novelty: none of the surveyed works select among multiple flow/diffusion-sampled action modes using the model's own anticipated-contact trajectory (§3, seed-selection); RDP and TacDiffusion both address multi-modality via architecture (asymmetric decoder, frequency filter) rather than via contact-anticipation-guided resampling.

## 6. Top 3 papers to read in full

1. **FeelWorld** (arXiv 2607.24267) — closest architectural analogue to our teacher (joint video/tactile world model, hierarchical gate + two-stage planner); best source of concrete gating/loss design details for §3.
2. **Reactive Diffusion Policy** (arXiv 2503.02881) — the clearest existing precedent for splitting a chunked generative policy into a slow "commit" mode and a fast tactile-reactive correction; maps directly onto our 0.9 s replan boundary.
3. **TACO** (arXiv 2607.02840) — best match for turning a small, auto-labeled rollout set into a self-improvement update without diluting the pretrained backbone; directly answers our rig-time/sample-budget constraint (50–150 rollouts, existing DAgger/AWR scaffolding).
