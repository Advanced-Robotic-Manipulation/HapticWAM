## 2. Best-evidenced use of our 45 failure demos + autonomous rollouts

The clearest, most convergent lesson across SSDF (2401.08957), "How to utilize failure demo data" (2605.07560), Set-Supervised Diffusion Policy (2606.01865), and Q-Planning (2608.21204) is: **zero-weighting the action loss on failure demos throws away the single most informative signal for exactly our bug** (the model never sees "having closed on air, the correct next action is *not* lift"). Every paper that reports a real gain does one of two things instead of dropping the action target: (a) reweight it continuously by a learned quality/advantage score (SSDF, AW-Opt, TOPReward), or (b) use it as an explicit *negative* to contrastively shape the desired output set (SDP), or (c) train a *separate* lightweight value head that consumes chunk + outcome and reweights samples from the frozen generator at inference (Q-Planning) — cleanly separating "what the flow model can generate" from "which of its own candidates should be trusted."

Given our stack (flow-matching chunk policy that already conditions on `prev_cpk`, and an ACC gate that already outputs a calibrated `p(contact)` — our own log shows `p_none=0.99` correctly flagging "nothing there"), **the Q-Planning pattern (2608.21204) is the best fit and the cheapest to add**, because it requires no change to the 2B-parameter backbone's training objective at all:

- **Minimal code-level change**: add a lightweight critic `Qφ(obs, prev_cpk, action_chunk) → scalar`, trained with a chunk-level Bellman/classification loss using terminal reward `r∈{0,1}` = autonomous success label (see §3). Training data = the 45 failure demos (label 0) + the 280 ordinary successes (label 1) + every rig rollout collected so far, both v4 and v5 (labels from tactile/OBJ, not humans). This is a few-hundred-step training job on frozen features (minutes, not an H100-day).
- At replan time, instead of drawing one flow-matching sample (NFE=5, one seed), draw **K=4–8 candidate chunks from different noise seeds** (we already observed "one noise seed descended at full speed to the right height" — i.e. good candidates already exist in the sampling distribution, they're just not being selected) and combine via the same softmax-weighted average `w⁽ⁿ⁾∝exp(Qφ/λ)`, or simply argmax-select for the first version. This directly attacks the reported multi-modality-across-seeds symptom without touching persistent-noise replanning or the backbone.
- As a second, slightly deeper change (worth doing if #1's gain is too small): add a scalar **outcome-conditioning token** into the same conditioning slot family as `prev_cpk` (e.g. `prev_outcome ∈ {success, fail, unknown}`), set it to `fail` for the 45 failure demos' action loss (now given a small positive weight, not 0) and `success` for all successful closes, matching the return-conditioning idea behind AW-Opt/RCP-style methods and Q-Planning's own reward-conditioning; at deployment, always force `prev_outcome=success`. This is a one-line change to the existing token-embedding table plus re-enabling the action loss (weight ~0.1–0.3, not full 1.0, à la SSDF's continuous quality weight rather than binary on/off) on the failure demos.

Both changes are compatible and additive; the critic-reweighting change is lower-risk (no backbone retrain) and should be tried first.

## 3. Success auto-labeling: signals and reliability

We do **not** need a VLM for this failure mode — the causal signal is already on the robot:
- **Robotiq 2F-85 `OBJ` register** (already read via `ur_state`'s gripper pos+OBJ field): `OBJ=2` (stopped, no object detected) at the end of the close action is a near-ground-truth failure label for "closed on air"; `OBJ=1` (stopped, object detected) + subsequent lift-height gain is a near-ground-truth success label. This is a standard, well-established signal in the self-supervised-grasping literature (search results above; multiple works report success determined by "gripper closure post-lift" at >90% reliability because it's a direct mechanical read, not a learned proxy).
- **DM-Tac gel contact area / force**, and our own **ACC gate `p(contact soon)`**, which our rig log already shows calibrated (`p_none=0.99` correctly matching "nothing there"). This is a stronger, earlier signal than OBJ because it fires *before* the close completes, matching what ARMADA's FLOAT detector (vision/force-based, ~95% accuracy, >20 pts over prior SOTA) and Q-Planning's per-episode terminal-reward labeling both rely on as their generic template — ours is more direct because it is tactile, not a visual proxy.
- **Lift-height + z-trajectory** as a tertiary check (did the arm actually rise with an object versus just transport an empty gripper) — cheap, already logged in `planner_trace.json`.
- VLM-based success classifiers (RoboFuME's cross-institution VLM reward, TOPReward's token-probability progress signal, RoboReward's purpose-built reward model) are the right tool when no privileged sensor exists, but for us they would be a strictly worse, higher-latency, higher-cost proxy for a signal (contact + OBJ) we already have at zero marginal cost. Recommend combining OBJ (mechanical ground truth at grasp) + ACC gate (early tactile prediction, for chunk-level reward during training) and reserving a VLM/vision end-of-episode check only for corner cases (e.g., object dropped in transit) that OBJ alone might miss.

## 4. Two-to-three-iteration self-improvement plan

Rig budget note from our own constraints: ~15–20 total rig-hours, 2 operators, one UR3; real-robot RL with hundreds of trials is out of budget, but 50–150 autonomously-labeled rollouts is exactly the range every real-robot paper above operated in.

- **Iteration 0 (already have this)**: the 08-28 session, 26 rollouts (v4+v5), all autonomously labelable post-hoc from `planner_trace.json` (OBJ + ACC gate + lift height) with zero extra rig time — this becomes the first critic-training batch.
- **Iteration 1**: train the chunk-level critic `Qφ` (§2) on {45 failure demos, 280 success demos, 26 iteration-0 rollouts}; deploy with K-candidate Q-reweighted sampling; collect 40–60 new rollouts across the 4 tasks (roughly matching Q-Planning's 20/task/iteration real-robot cadence, doubled since we have 4 tasks not 1–2). Label autonomously.
- **Iteration 2**: fold the new rollouts in (~100 total labeled episodes, matching Q-Planning's reported budget for its headline +50-point gains on stack-cups/insert-wallet), retrain the critic (a few hundred steps, no backbone retrain), redeploy, re-measure endpoint success and descent-speed/height statistics. If a third iteration is affordable within remaining rig-hours, repeat with the enlarged pool; otherwise report the 2-iteration curve as the paper's ablation, which is already sufficient to show a monotonic trend (Q-Planning demonstrates most of its gain within the first 2–3 iterations on the reported curves).

## 5. Novelty angle

The self-improvement literature (Q-Planning, ARMADA, SIRIUS, RoboFuME) all bolt an *external* success/value signal onto a policy that has no internal notion of contact. PHANTOM already trains an anticipatory contact classifier as a first-class world-model output, supervised by real tactile ground truth. Using **the model's own calibrated contact-anticipation confidence as the reward/label for a Q-Planning-style residual self-improvement stage** — rather than a bolted-on VLM classifier (RoboFuME/TOPReward/RoboReward) or a generic vision/force failure detector (ARMADA) — is a genuinely new combination, and it is the natural next sentence after the paper's own thesis ("anticipating contact from vision+proprio, taught by touch, closes the loop on grasping"): here, that same anticipated-contact signal closes the loop not just at inference-time gating, but at training-time self-improvement, with no additional sensors, no VLM cost, and no full backbone retrain.

## 6. Top 3 papers to read in full

1. **Q-Planning (2608.21204)** — closest mechanism match: chunk-level value function reweighting candidates from a frozen flow-matching policy, exactly our architecture's shape.
2. **ARMADA (2510.02298)** — for the autonomous-failure-detection design (FLOAT) and the detector-triggered shared-control data-acquisition loop, as the template for our OBJ+ACC-based auto-labeling pipeline.
3. **Set-Supervised Diffusion Policy (2606.01865)** — for the exact contrastive "desired set" loss formulation, to adapt our 45 failure demos as one-sided negatives against the matched successful closes at the same task/pose.
