# Closed-loop execution of action-chunk policies — literature review for PHANTOM

## 1. Papers table

| Title / arXiv id / year | Method (one paragraph) | Key real-robot numbers | Sample/compute budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Real-Time Chunking (RTC)**, 2506.07339, 2025 (Black, Galliker, Levine — Physical Intelligence) | Inference-time-only algorithm for any diffusion/flow action-chunk policy. On every replan, the first `d` actions (d = inference-delay steps) are **frozen** (guaranteed to execute), the last `s` actions of the new chunk (non-overlapping) get weight 0, and the middle region gets an **exponentially decaying soft mask** `w_i=(e^{c_i}-1)/(e-1)`, `c_i=(H-s-i)/(H-s-d+1)`. Instead of literally reusing the frozen actions, a **ΠGDM-style guidance term** is added to the flow's velocity field at each denoising step (with guidance-weight clipping β=5 for NFE≈5 stability) so the new sample is *inpainted* to agree with the frozen prefix while still being freshly resampled — no raw noise persistence, a fresh Gaussian is drawn every replan. | π0.5 VLA, 6 bimanual tasks, 480 real episodes / 28 h robot time. RTC: no degradation up to +200 ms injected delay; synchronous execution degrades linearly; naive temporal-ensembling variants **trigger the robot's protective e-stop from oscillation** at high delay. RTC completes tasks ~20% faster than synchronous baseline. Inference overhead: 97 ms vs 76 ms vanilla (RTX 4090), NFE=5. | Zero extra training — pure inference-time algorithm on a pretrained flow/diffusion VLA. |
| **Bidirectional Decoding (BID)**, 2408.17355, ICLR 2025 | At each replan, samples **N=16** candidate chunks and keeps the top **K=3** by likelihood, then scores each by (i) **backward coherence** — weighted L2 distance to the previously committed chunk (Σ ρ^τ‖a^t_{t+τ}−a^{t-1}_{t+τ}‖) and (ii) **forward contrast** — distance to a strong-policy positive set minus distance to a weak-policy negative set — and executes the best-scoring chunk. Tested on Diffusion Policy and VQ-BeT. | Push-T, RoboMimic (5 tasks), Franka Kitchen, plus 2 real tasks with a **moving target** (dynamic placing/picking): BID gives substantially higher success than the vanilla chunked baseline, **2× success rate** on dynamic cup-pulling. Explicitly diagnoses that "when successive chunks follow distinct latent strategies, averaging them may not yield a plausible strategy" — i.e. naive chunk blending across modes can produce an incoherent, in-between action. | Inference-time only; cost = N forward samples per replan (16 in the paper) vs. 1. No retraining. |
| **Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT)**, 2304.13705, RSS 2023 (Zhao et al., ALOHA) | Predicts a k-step action chunk; **temporal ensembling** re-queries the policy at every timestep and exponentially averages the overlapping predictions with weight `w_i=exp(-m·i)` (i=0 is the oldest/most-committed prediction), trading off jerkiness (small k) vs. compounding error (large k). | Real bimanual ALOHA tasks, 80–90% success from 10 min of demos. **Chunk-length ablation is an inverted U**: ~1% success at k=1 (fully reactive, no chunking) rising to ~44% at k=100, then declining again at k=200–400 (near fully open-loop). Temporal ensembling adds +3.3–4% on top of the best k. | 10 minutes of teleop demos per task; standard imitation-learning compute. |
| **Causal Confusion in Imitation Learning**, 1905.11979, NeurIPS 2019 (de Haan, Jayaraman, Levine) | Shows BC is non-causal: conditioning on more of the observation history can make closed-loop performance *worse* under distribution shift because the policy can key off a spurious, non-causal correlate. Canonical example: a dashcam brake-light indicator is an *effect* of braking, not a cause; a policy that conditions on it learns to brake only after the light is already on, i.e. it **freezes** (never initiates braking) once deployed, because in closed loop the light never appears until the (never-taken) action has already happened. Fix = targeted intervention: train a mixture of policies over candidate causal-graph hypotheses, disambiguate with a handful of expert queries or on-policy rollouts (tens of trials, not hundreds). | Simulated driving + MuJoCo continuous control; the paper's contribution is diagnostic/methodological, not large-scale real-robot benchmarking. | Tens of targeted intervention trials (far cheaper than DAgger-scale relabeling). |
| **Fighting Copycat Agents / the Copycat Problem**, 2010.14876, NeurIPS 2020 (Wen et al.) | Formalizes the "copycat" instance of causal confusion: when (a) expert actions are strongly autocorrelated and (b) the previous action is recoverable from the observation history, a history-conditioned BC policy learns to just **replay its last action** rather than react — this minimizes training loss almost everywhere except exactly at the moments the behavior must *change* (e.g. brake→throttle at a green light), which is precisely where it then fails in deployment. Fix: a Target-Conditioned Adversary (TCA) that predicts the previous action *conditioned on the current action*, paired with an information bottleneck, so only the "cheating" nuisance correlate is scrubbed from the representation, not everything useful. | MuJoCo (Walker2D, Hopper, Ant, etc.) — a "copycat" agent's right knee **repeats the previous action and never transitions** from extended to bent. Authors note image-based gains are smaller than state-based gains. No real-robot arm results reported. | Offline-only method, no extra environment interaction required for training. |
| **Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (DPPO)**, 2409.00588, ICLR 2025 | Fine-tunes a pretrained BC diffusion policy with policy-gradient RL by treating the denoising chain itself as an inner MDP nested inside the environment MDP (a "two-layer" diffusion-policy MDP), giving structured, on-manifold exploration. Directly targets exactly the failure mode where an offline-good BC diffusion policy is not robust/smooth in closed loop. | Sim-trained policy deployed **zero-shot** on real hardware for a long-horizon multi-stage assembly task, and separately improves pretrained diffusion policies substantially on Furniture-Bench multi-stage assembly from sparse reward alone. | RL fine-tuning on top of an existing pretrained diffusion policy — no need to retrain from scratch; the natural next step after BC when closed-loop ≠ open-loop performance. |
| **Consistency Policy**, 2405.07503, RSS 2024 | Distills a pretrained Diffusion Policy into a consistency model that generates an action chunk in ~1 step via self-consistency along the teacher's ODE trajectories, cutting inference latency by roughly an order of magnitude vs. the fastest diffusion baseline while staying competitive on success rate, runnable on a laptop GPU. | 3 real-world tasks + 6 sim tasks; competitive success rates at much lower latency; robust to teacher-model quality variation. | Distillation of an already-trained diffusion policy; no new real-world data needed. |
| **Diff-Control (stateful diffusion policy)**, 2404.12539, 2024 | Diagnoses that vanilla diffusion policy is memoryless across replans and thus multi-modal/inconsistent chunk-to-chunk; adds a **ControlNet-style side branch** that explicitly conditions generation on the *previously executed* actions (a Bayesian transition-model formulation), rather than relying on raw proprioception passed into the same backbone to implicitly encode history. | Real robot: language-conditioned kitchen 92% vs. 88% (vanilla DP); high-precision lid-opening 80% vs. 64% (+16 pts); dynamic duck-scooping 84% vs. 76%; **periodic drum-beat task 72% vs. 24% (+48 pts)**; ≥30% higher success under physical perturbation with recovery behavior. | Adds one small side-network to an existing diffusion policy; standard real-robot data budget, no extra demos needed beyond the base policy's. |
| **RA-DP (Rapid Adaptive Diffusion Policy)**, 2503.04051, 2025 | Training-free, high-frequency replanning for diffusion policies via **noise persistence / warm-starting**: instead of starting each replan's denoising from fresh Gaussian noise, it carries over and adapts the previous chunk's noise trajectory, removing the "cold start" that otherwise causes visible **hesitation** at every replan boundary. Uses loss-based guidance for adaptation. | Manipulation tasks with measurably higher achievable replan frequency and success vs. standard DP replanning; paper frames standard diffusion replanning's cold-start denoising as the direct cause of hesitation between chunks. | Training-free / inference-time only. |
| **Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP)**, 2503.02881, 2025 | **Slow-fast hierarchy** for contact-rich tasks: a slow latent diffusion policy predicts high-level action chunks from vision at low frequency; a fast, separate tactile-reactive tokenizer/controller runs at high frequency to react to tactile feedback *within* a chunk's execution — explicitly built because chunked action prediction "lacks the ability to respond instantly to real-time tactile feedback during the chunk." | 3 contact-rich real tasks; RDP "significantly improves performance" vs. state-of-the-art visual-IL baselines (exact numbers behind the full text, but architecture and motivation map directly onto our problem). | Two-network hierarchy trained jointly on the same demo set as the base policy; no extra data volume implied. |
| **Mixture of Horizons (MoH) in Action Chunking**, 2511.19433, 2025 | Identifies an intrinsic trade-off in action-chunk horizon: longer horizons give better global foresight but degrade fine-grained precision, shorter horizons sharpen local control but hurt long-horizon tasks. MoH splits the chunk into several parallel segments of *different* horizons processed by a shared transformer and fused with a lightweight learned gate, getting both foresight and precision from one model, "plug-and-play" with minimal extra training/inference cost. | Reported gains on manipulation benchmarks/generalization to complex long-horizon tasks (VLA-scale); minimal overhead vs. full-attention single-horizon baselines. | Small architectural add-on (gating head) on top of an existing chunked transformer; modest extra training, no extra demos. |
| **CFG-DP (Classifier-Free Guidance for Diffusion Policy)**, 2510.09786, 2025 | Applies classifier-free guidance (blending conditional/unconditional model predictions with a guidance factor) to a diffusion policy specifically to sharpen temporal coherence and reduce **"repetitive actions"** — i.e., stuck/hesitating/back-and-forth behavior — by tuning the guidance strength as a pure inference-time knob. | Real humanoid robot: "high success rates and minimal repetitive actions" reported; guidance factor tuned empirically to balance coherence vs. accuracy (exact scale values not extracted from available text, but the mechanism and target failure mode — repetitive/stuck actions — directly match ours). | Inference-time hyperparameter sweep on an already-trained CFG-capable policy; effectively free. |

## 2. What explains OUR symptoms

Our symptoms, restated: the policy **decelerates and stops 65–120 mm above the object**, closes on air, and — despite the tactile gate correctly reporting p(contact)≈0 (p_none 0.99) — **lifts and retries** instead of continuing to descend; rollouts are ~30% slower than demos; **one noise seed** at replan reached full speed and the correct height while others stalled (seed-level multimodality); and the *same checkpoint* scores well (17 mm endpoint error) when evaluated open-loop on held-out demo states. The failure-demo data (45 under-grasp episodes) carries zero action-loss weight, so the action head has literally never been trained on "what to do when a close attempt is failing."

**(a) This is a closed-loop-specific, not a capacity, failure — textbook covariate shift compounded by causal confusion / the copycat problem.** de Haan et al. (1905.11979) and Wen et al. (2010.14876) describe exactly this pattern: a history-conditioned policy discovers a training-time shortcut (predict next action ≈ f(own recent state/action)) that is nearly loss-free on the training distribution — because demos are smooth, monotone descents where velocity at t predicts velocity at t+1 almost perfectly — but is *non-causal*. Once closed-loop replanning introduces even small deviations from the training trajectory (imperfect chunk blending, a slightly slow-mode noise sample, 0.9 s replan latency), the model reads back its own now-slightly-slower `tcp_speed`/`ur_state` and predicts *further* deceleration, because "slow arm → slow next chunk" was a valid — but purely correlational — pattern in training. This is a self-reinforcing feedback loop toward zero velocity, i.e. a freezing/copycat failure, and it explains why the *same weights* are fine open-loop (evaluated on true demo states, where the spurious correlate still holds) but fail closed-loop (where the correlate has been violated by the policy's own prior actions). The causal-confusion framing also explains why the failure is invisible in the offline metric: "access to more information [own proprio history] yields worse generalization under distribution shift," precisely because the offline metric never exercises the shifted state distribution.

**(b) The contact gate not being wired to velocity is an architectural, not a data, gap.** Reactive Diffusion Policy (2503.02881) diagnoses the identical structural problem: chunked, jointly-generated action+contact prediction "lacks the ability to respond instantly to real-time [tactile] feedback during the chunk." Our ACC gate's p_none=0.99 is correct and available in real time, but it currently only re-enters the model as `prev_cpk` context for the *next* full generative replan (0.9 s later) — it has no path to directly govern velocity or veto a stop/lift decision mid-chunk. That is exactly the gap RDP's fast reactive layer is built to close.

**(c) Seed-level multimodality with no selection mechanism is a known, previously-solved failure of chunk sampling.** BID (2408.17355) explicitly documents that independently-sampled chunks can land in different latent "modes" and that blending across modes ("when successive chunks follow distinct latent strategies, averaging them may not yield a plausible strategy") produces incoherent, damped behavior rather than either mode cleanly — which matches "chunk blending" possibly averaging a fast-commit mode with a slow-check mode into something that is neither. Our current stack samples one chunk per replan (persistent noise, RA-DP-style) with no scoring/selection across candidates, so whichever mode the noise seed lands in each 0.9 s window is what gets executed — consistent with the observation that one seed reached full speed/correct height and others didn't.

**(d) Chunk-horizon length trades off foresight vs. precision.** Mixture-of-Horizons (2511.19433) and ACT's inverted-U ablation (2304.13705: ~1%@k=1 → ~44%@k=100 → decline at k=200-400) both show that a single fixed, relatively long horizon (ours: 16 steps @ 10 Hz = 1.6 s, vs. a 0.9 s replan cadence, i.e. ~44% chunk overlap per replan) can under-serve fine-grained, near-contact precision even while it is well-suited to the global reach-and-descend phase — plausibly contributing to "commits to the wrong terminal height" behavior at exactly the last few centimeters where precision matters most.

**(e) Noise-persistence (our current approach) is not obviously the state-of-the-art way to keep replans smooth, and could itself be compounding the stall.** RA-DP (2503.04051) validates that warm-starting/persisting noise across replans avoids the "cold-start hesitation" of resampling from scratch — but RTC (2506.07339) shows a materially more robust alternative: rather than reusing the same noise trajectory, RTC draws **fresh** noise every replan and instead enforces continuity via an explicit *guidance* term (soft-masked inpainting) toward the already-committed action prefix. If our persisted noise trajectory has itself settled into a "slow" mode, warm-starting from it every 0.9 s could be actively re-committing to that mode rather than giving the sampler a chance to escape it — a mechanistic account for why the stall, once entered, does not self-correct within an episode.

## 3. Ranked recommendations (minimal change to our stack, expected effect)

1. **Add BID-style multi-sample scoring at replan time (highest expected value, no retraining).** With NFE=5 already, sample K=4–8 candidate chunks per 0.9 s replan (cheap relative to the Cosmos-2B backbone's dominant cost) and score them by (i) coherence with the already-committed prefix and (ii) forward progress — reuse the existing ACC gate/contact-package output as the "forward" signal (e.g. penalize chunks whose predicted descent stalls while p_none stays high) instead of BID's learned positive/negative policy sets. *Expected effect*: converts our seed-lottery (~1-in-N chance of the "fast" mode) into a reliable selection, directly targeting the observed seed multimodality. Verifiable **offline** first, by re-scoring the logged `planner_trace.json` chunks from the 08-28 session before spending rig time.

2. **Wire the ACC gate into a fast velocity governor/override (RDP-style), not just prev_cpk context.** Minimal version: while p_none stays high and z is above the target grasp height, prevent the existing sigma-based speed governor from decaying velocity below a floor, or force continued descent at the last known good rate. *Expected effect*: directly breaks the pathology where the model "confidently sees nothing" (p_none 0.99) yet still stops and lifts — this is the most literal fix for the reported symptom and requires no retraining, only a runtime guard in code we already have (speed governor / chunk blending).

3. **Diagnose and, if confirmed, break the proprio-velocity shortcut (causal confusion / copycat check).** Cheap diagnostic: at inference, ablate/zero `tcp_speed` (and possibly `qd`) in proprio for a subset of rollouts, à la de Haan et al.'s targeted intervention, and see whether stalling disappears or worsens. If confirmed as the mechanism, the Diff-Control-style fix (2404.12539) — moving "own recent action" conditioning into an explicit, separately-gated side-branch (ControlNet-like) rather than raw concatenation into `ur_state` — gave the largest single-technique real-robot gains in this review (+16 to +48 points on precision/periodic tasks) for a comparably small architecture change on top of an existing diffusion/flow policy.

4. **Sweep classifier-free guidance scale above 1.0 (near-zero-cost experiment).** CFG-DP (2510.09786) reports that tuning guidance strength specifically reduces "repetitive/stuck" behavior on a real robot. This is a pure inference-time parameter — testable with a handful of rig episodes or even offline re-scoring of existing rollouts if guidance can be recomputed from logged conditioning features, before touching anything else.

5. **Replace raw noise persistence with RTC-style guided inpainting for the 0.9 s replan.** Medium engineering effort (implement the ΠGDM guidance term with the paper's β=5 clipping for NFE≈5 stability) but directly targets risk (e) above — a fresh sample every replan, steered toward continuity by guidance rather than by literally re-denoising the same (possibly stalled) noise trajectory, is the best-evidenced fix for latency-induced smoothness in the literature (RTC shows zero degradation up to 200 ms injected delay where naive approaches oscillate into an e-stop).

6. **Medium-term: DPPO-style policy-gradient fine-tuning / the already-planned terminal-phase residual RL**, using the tactile-derived success signal as reward over the ~50–150 feasible rig rollouts. This is exactly the regime DPPO (2409.00588) targets — an offline-good BC diffusion policy that needs closed-loop robustness, fixed by RL fine-tuning on top of the pretrained backbone rather than retraining from scratch — and matches the sample budget already identified as feasible for PHANTOM's rig time.

## 4. Risks

- **Compute budget for multi-sample scoring (rec. 1):** K=4–8 samples at NFE=5 through a 2B-parameter action-conditioned video DiT may not fit the 0.9 s replan budget on one GPU; needs profiling before committing rig time. If too slow, fall back to K=2–3 or amortize by scoring only every other replan.
- **Velocity-governor override (rec. 2) is a safety-relevant change:** a confidently-wrong gate (false p_none) combined with a forced-descent override risks a harder/faster contact than intended; must keep the existing z-floor and STOP hitbox as hard limits and treat this as an additive floor on velocity, not a replacement for the learned policy's own deceleration where it's correct (e.g. near a truly present object).
- **Causal-confusion diagnosis/fix (rec. 3) costs a retrain/fine-tune cycle** — affordable in GPU-hours (~$2–3/h H100) but adds calendar days against the ~3-week deadline; should be run in parallel with, not blocking, recs 1/2/4 which need no retraining.
- **RTC-style guidance reimplementation (rec. 5) is nontrivial under deadline pressure** and the paper's own ablations show it's unstable without the exact β-clipping safeguard at low NFE — a partial/buggy port could be worse than the current noise-persistence baseline; treat as a stretch goal, not a near-term fix.
- **All recommendations compete for the same 15–20 scarce rig-hours.** Recs 1, 2, and 4 should be validated as much as possible offline (re-scoring logged `planner_trace.json` chunks, sweeping guidance on cached conditioning) before any of them consume rig time, reserving live trials for confirming the winning combination.
- **None of this addresses the deeper data gap** — the 45 failure demos carry no action-loss signal, so no amount of inference-time fixing teaches the model a *causal* "what to do after a failed close" behavior; recs 3 and 6 are the only items here that touch that root cause (via representation fixes or RL reward, respectively) rather than papering over it at inference time.

## 5. Top 3 papers to read in full

1. **RTC — Real-Time Execution of Action Chunking Flow Policies (2506.07339).** Closest architectural match to our exact stack (flow-matching action chunks, ~1 s-scale replan cadence, chunk-boundary smoothness, NFE≈5) and the strongest evidence in this review that raw noise persistence is not the ceiling for closed-loop smoothness — read in full for the exact guidance-term derivation and the β-clipping stability fix before attempting rec. 5.
2. **BID — Bidirectional Decoding (2408.17355).** The single highest-expected-value, zero-retraining fix for our most concretely observed symptom (seed-level multimodality); read in full for the exact backward-coherence/forward-contrast scoring so it can be adapted to use our own ACC gate/contact-package signal as the "forward" term.
3. **Causal Confusion in Imitation Learning (1905.11979).** The most likely mechanistic explanation for *why* the policy decelerates specifically in closed loop while scoring well open-loop; read in full for the targeted-intervention protocol (tens of trials, not hundreds) as a rig-budget-compatible way to confirm or rule out the proprio-velocity shortcut before investing in an architectural fix (Diff-Control-style side branch).
