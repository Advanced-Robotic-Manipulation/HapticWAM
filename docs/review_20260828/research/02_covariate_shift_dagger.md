## 2. What fits a 3-week, 1-rig, 2-operator, 50–150-rollout budget with automatic tactile success labels

Quantitatively, the two closest real-manipulation analogs (CR-DAgger 2506.16685, TER-DAgger 2603.04038) both converged on **50–100 correction episodes per task** producing 37–64 point absolute success-rate jumps, using base-demo counts (150–405) comparable to ours (250/task). That number sits comfortably inside a 50–150 rollout budget for a *single* task (waffles, say, as the reviewer-facing hero task), and even across all 4 tasks split proportionally (~15–35 corrections/task) is defensible if you concentrate correction data on the task(s) you'll report rig numbers for.

FlowDAgger (2607.08877) is the most architecturally relevant: it assumes exactly a generative/flow-matching action head with a noise-conditioned output — structurally identical to our rectified-flow chunk head with persistent noise across replans — and gets large gains from **5–20** correction episodes without any weight update to the base 2B-param backbone (train only a small noise-steering module). This is the cheapest path to try first: it needs no LoRA re-training loop, no H100 rental, and can be iterated same-day on the rig.

Q-Planning's finding that **filtered-SFT (success-only relabeling) plateaus** (55%/30% vs. their 90%/80%) is the key negative result for planning your own 50–150-rollout budget: pure "collect autonomous rollouts, keep the successes, retrain" will NOT close a gap this large (0/13 → 0/26) on its own. Some human-corrective signal (à la CR-DAgger/Sirius/FlowDAgger) needs to be in the loop, not just autonomous filtering.

Given automatic tactile success labels (which we already have via the gate + terminal metric), the best-fit recipe is **auto-triggered HG-DAgger**: use the ACC gate itself (p_contact) exactly the way TER-DAgger uses predicted-vs-measured force discrepancy — trigger a human takeover automatically when p_none stays high while height crosses below the historical grasp-height band and descent velocity has decayed below the demo distribution. This converts "operator watches and reacts" (slow, fatigue-prone) into "system flags the exact 65–120 mm stall window" (matches the diagnosed failure precisely), letting 2 operators run more corrections per rig-hour.

## 3. Can the 325 "recovery" demos be salvaged?

As designed, the 45 under-grasp-failure demos (`action_weight=0`, contact heads supervised) **cannot** teach the fix for the observed bug, and this is diagnostically important: the bug is that the model *correctly* predicts p_none≈0.99 (contact heads are fine — they were supervised) but doesn't act on it (action head never learned a "close failed, continue descending / retry" behavior, because action loss was zeroed exactly on the trajectories where that behavior occurs). This is a clean instance of the causal-confusion result (1905.11979): the model has a channel carrying the right information (the gate) but no training signal ever taught the action head to *use* it, so at deployment the action head extrapolates to the closest thing it has seen (the demo-average "close now" behavior) regardless of what the gate says.

Salvage path (cheap, do first, ~0 rig-hours): audit the raw teleop logs behind the 45 fail-tagged episodes for whether the operator continued past the failed close (regrasp, deeper descent, retry). If yes: relabel that continuation segment with real action supervision (restore action_weight there) and re-run the ~3k-step v5-style fine-tune — this is free in rig time, only needs GPU-hours, and should be tried before spending any of the 15–20 rig-hours. If no continuation was recorded (operator just tagged "fail" and lifted, per the current pipeline description), the 45 episodes are only useful as **hard-negative / risk-region labels** — feed them into a ThriftyDAgger-style (2109.08273) risk/novelty detector to prioritize *where* to spend the next 50–100 correction rollouts (i.e., use them to aim the new data collection, not to train the action head directly). Either way, this is a half-day desk-check before committing rig time — do it first.

The 280 ordinary successes remain fully usable as-is; no change needed there.

## 4. Concrete recipe, ranked by expected rig-impact per day

1. **(Day 0, desk-only, 0 rig-hrs):** Audit the 45 fail-tagged trajectories for a recorded recovery continuation; if present, relabel + restore action loss, re-fine-tune on existing GPU rental. Highest expected value per hour spent because it's free in rig time.
2. **(Day 1, ~2 rig-hrs, cheap diagnostic):** Multi-seed sweep to confirm/quantify the multimodal-velocity finding (one seed already descended correctly). Build a lightweight gate-gated seed-selection wrapper (pick the noise seed whose predicted trajectory keeps descending / has lower predicted p_none at grasp height) — zero retraining, testable same day. This is speculative but nearly free; treat as a spike, not the primary bet.
3. **(Days 2–5, ~8–10 rig-hrs, 2 operators):** FlowDAgger-style (2607.08877) or CR-DAgger-style (2506.16685) correction collection: **50–100 episodes**, auto-triggered by the ACC gate at the diagnosed 65–120 mm stall band (TER-DAgger-style auto-trigger, 2603.04038), teleop pushes through to a tactile-confirmed close. This is the best-evidenced lever (37–64 point gains at this exact budget in the literature) and directly targets the observed failure geometry.
4. **(Day 5–6, ~2–4 rig-hrs):** Top up with 30–50 autonomous rollouts of the corrected policy, auto-labeled by the tactile success signal, folded in with **Sirius/IWR-style reweighting** (2211.08416) rather than hard filtering — ThriftyDAgger/Sirius both show reweighting beats binary include/exclude, and Q-Planning (2608.21204) shows filtering alone plateaus, so don't rely on this step in isolation.
5. **(Optional, 1 rig-day, side experiment):** Proprio/velocity dropout ablation inspired by 2509.18644 (which got 0%→85% and 6%→64% OOD gains purely from removing proprio reliance) — fine-tune a variant with proprio velocity features dropped or heavily noised, test whether full-speed committed descent returns. High information value for the paper's diagnosis section even if not adopted for the final policy. Do NOT invest in the Wasserstein-noise variant (2506.23944) — it's withdrawn/unvalidated.

Training-recipe changes to make regardless of which data-collection step above you run: (a) never zero action loss on a "failure" segment that contains any real corrective action — supervise it fully; (b) reweight (IWR/Sirius-style) rather than binary-filter when mixing autonomous rollouts back in; (c) treat the ACC gate as an actionable auto-DAgger trigger signal, not just an auxiliary loss target — it already carries the information needed to catch this exact failure the moment it happens.

## 5. Risks

- 50–100 corrections is the *low* end of what published DAgger-variant papers used per task; if the root cause is architectural (proprio/velocity self-consistency bias) rather than missing local corrective states, DAgger-style corrections may raise the rate without fully closing the gap — hence pairing step 3 with the proprio-ablation spike (step 5) rather than betting everything on one lever.
- If the 45 fail-tagged episodes have no recorded recovery continuation, the salvage step (item 1) yields nothing free and the full 15–20 rig-hour budget must go to fresh corrections.
- Teleop correction quality varies with 2 operators/fatigue over multiple days — CR-DAgger and HG-DAgger both stress that handoff/correction quality control matters as much as quantity.
- FlowDAgger's noise-space correction trick and the seed-selection wrapper are unvalidated on our exact tactile+flow-matching architecture — budget them as 1-day spikes, not guaranteed fixes.
- For reviewers: frame any DAgger-lite phase as necessary engineering to make the sensor-free-student baseline comparison fair, not as a paper contribution in itself — the closest published analogs (CR-DAgger, TER-DAgger, Sirius) are well-trodden, so claim novelty on the tactile-anticipation side, not on the covariate-shift fix.

## 6. Top 3 to read in full

1. **Compliant Residual DAgger, 2506.16685** — closest direct analog: same correction-budget regime (50–100/task), same "policy keeps running while human corrects" pattern we'd need with a single rig.
2. **Force-Aware Residual DAgger / TER-DAgger, 2603.04038** — gives a ready template for auto-triggering intervention from a predicted-vs-actual discrepancy signal, structurally identical to using our ACC gate as the trigger.
3. **Causal Confusion in Imitation Learning, 1905.11979** — theoretical grounding for the exact observed bug (gate correctly says "no contact," action head ignores it) and the general framing for why `prev_cpk` history-feedback needs care (copycat/shortcut risk) in future iterations.
