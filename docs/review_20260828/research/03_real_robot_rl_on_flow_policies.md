## 2. Feasibility matrix for OUR budget (~50–150 real rollouts, automatic tactile reward, 3 weeks, one UR3 rig)

| Method | Runnable in our setting? | Why / why not |
|---|---|---|
| DPPO | **No** | Needs a sim environment matched to our task + full-weight PG through a 2B-param DiT with LoRA; their own real-robot number is a zero-shot eval, not a real-RL recipe. Building an Isaac-Sim replica of a tactile grasp task in 3 weeks is out of scope. |
| ReinFlow | **No** | Sim-only paper, no real-robot evidence anywhere; also fine-tunes the whole flow network's weights via PG — with our 2B-param backbone + LoRA this is an expensive, high-variance, not-yet-real-robot-validated bet. |
| Policy Decorator | **No (as published)** | Sim-only; also needs 10^5–10^6 env steps, several orders of magnitude beyond our rig-hour budget. The *idea* (bounded residual, ramped exploration schedule) is reusable, but the paper gives us no real-robot sample-efficiency evidence. |
| ResiP | **Partially — not in our timeline** | The actual RL step happens *in simulation*, with domain randomization and re-rendering; the "real" policy is a freshly retrained BC network, not our fine-tuned WAM. We have no sim of our tactile rig. Structurally elegant (frozen BC + closed-loop residual) but the pipeline (sim RL → synthetic re-render → re-distillation) needs infrastructure we don't have time to build. |
| **DSRL** | **Yes — best fit by sample budget** | 40–150 real episodes reported across 3 different real-robot setups (Franka, WidowX, π0), fully matches our 50–150 rollout budget, needs only black-box access to the frozen policy + a small actor-critic over the noise. Main uncertainty: our flow noise spans a *joint* video+action+contact sequence, not action-only, so noise-space RL needs to be scoped to just the action-token noise slice — an engineering risk, not a sample-budget risk. |
| **ResFiT** | **Yes — best fit by mechanism match** | 134–343 real rollouts, off-policy, on top of a *frozen chunked BC policy* (exactly our situation), sparse binary reward (which we can automate via our tactile gate instead of ResFiT's human labeling). Acts directly on the executed action, sidestepping any coupling with the video/noise machinery. Slightly above our upper budget (150) but the WoollyBallPnP task hit 64% success in 134 episodes/~15 min — scalable down if we restrict the residual to a low-dim subspace (see §3). |
| ZPRL | **Marginal — time risk** | Real-robot, frozen flow-matching base (architecturally closest to ours), automatable reward is plausible — but wall-clock cost (3.5–12.5 h *per task*) would eat most or all of our 15–20 total rig-hours for a single task, leaving nothing for the A/B and trace-decomposition work already planned. Only attempt if DSRL/ResFiT stall. |
| IBRL / HIL-SERL / SERL / RLPD | **No** | All assume (or build) a compact reactive policy trained from scratch with a replay buffer and hundreds of episodes; none are built to sit on top of a large pretrained generative chunk policy. Retrofitting our 2B DiT into their actor-critic loop is a bigger engineering lift than DSRL/ResFiT, for no corresponding gain — but their *infrastructure ideas* (automatic reward classifiers, async actor/learner, autonomous resets) are worth borrowing regardless of which residual method we pick. |

## 3. Recommended method + concrete recipe

**Bet: ResFiT-style low-dimensional residual RL on the terminal descent/close phase, trained live on the rig with automatic tactile reward, off-policy (REDQ/DDPG-style), 100–150 rollouts.** Treat DSRL-style noise-space steering as a lower-engineering-cost fallback/complement, given we already carry persistent noise across replans.

Why residual-on-executed-action over noise-steering as the primary bet: our diagnosed failure is not "the policy is generally incompetent," it's a **specific, temporally-localized decision** — stopping the descent and closing 65–120 mm early. A residual applied *after* the flow decodes an action can directly target exactly that decision without touching the 2B-param backbone, without worrying about how a noise perturbation propagates through the joint video/contact/action tokens, and without needing a simulator. DSRL's noise-space action for us would perturb a vector that also conditions video-token generation and the ACE contact heads — an entanglement risk ResFiT's approach avoids entirely.

**Concrete recipe:**

- **Action space for RL**: not the full 7-D Δ-EE+gripper action. Restrict the residual to the 2–3 dims implicated by the diagnosis: (a) a scalar bias on commanded descent velocity/Δz for the last ~1/3 of the chunk (to counter the observed 33% under-speed and the "stop early" bias), and (b) a scalar shift on the gripper-close trigger threshold/timing (the model treats "aperture matches a demo-like value" as its close criterion — nudging *when* it fires directly targets the "closes on air" bug). Optionally (c) a small bias on the speed-governor's sigma-based cap, since the current speed governor may itself be suppressing commit behavior. Keep the residual bounded (Policy Decorator's ±α idea) to something like ±15 mm/s and ±50 ms so a bad early update can't send the arm into the table.
- **Reward**: automatic, from signal the WAM already computes — no human labeling needed (this is the one place our tactile teacher pays for itself here). Define success as: ACC gate transitions p_contact>0.5 within a commanded-height band matching the demo close height (z≈72 mm ±tolerance), AND the terminal ACE wrench/CoP package indicates a sustained grasp (not a bump-and-release), AND (ground truth) object is lifted off the table (can be checked via the existing terminal-metric / gripper-OBJ signal already in `ur_state`). Reward 0 for early-close-on-air (p_none stays high through close) or for the known invalid-start / OOD-joint episodes (discard, don't reward-shape). This mirrors ResFiT's/ZPRL's sparse binary reward but swaps their human-in-the-loop labeling for the tactile signal PHANTOM already produces.
- **Rollouts per iteration**: small batches of 10–15 real rollouts between off-policy updates (REDQ-style high UTD ratio off the growing replay buffer), consistent with ResFiT's cadence (134 rollouts total, ~15 min for their smaller single-arm task). Budget: ~100–150 rollouts total ≈ 3–6 rig-hours including resets — leaves the rest of the 15–20 available rig-hours for the planned A/B against v4/v5 and trace decomposition.
- **What is retrained**: only a tiny MLP (state: proprio + predicted p_contact/cpk + commanded chunk; output: the 2–3-D bounded residual) plus its Q-ensemble critic. The 2B DiT backbone, LoRA weights, and tactile heads stay completely frozen — this is a deployment-time patch, not a new training run of the WAM.
- **Fallback/complement**: if the residual can't fully override the mode-collapse (i.e., if the "slow mode" is picked upstream by the persistent noise seed rather than correctable downstream), add a cheap DSRL-lite step — a tiny learned bias added only to the action-token slice of the persistent noise (not the video/contact slices), same reward, same off-policy critic. This is more engineering risk but directly attacks a seed-level mode-selection bug that a post-hoc action residual cannot.

## 4. How this reads to ICRA reviewers

The risk is real: "add residual RL fine-tuning on the rig" pattern-matches to ResFiT/ResiP/ZPRL and could read as a minor incremental application rather than a contribution. Two things make it defensible as a paper *result* rather than a paper *contribution* framing problem:
1. **Frame it as an ablation/diagnostic result, not a method contribution.** The RL fine-tune is not proposed as novel; it is evidence *for* the main WAM/anticipatory-contact claim — it shows the deployment gap is a covariate-shift/commit-timing bug (fixed by a tiny, cheap patch), not a perception or contact-modeling failure of the tactile teacher/student pipeline, which is the actual paper claim under test.
2. **The reward is free because of the paper's contribution.** A vision-only baseline has no automatic, real-time success signal at grasp time and would need human-labeled rewards (as ResFiT/ZPRL/HIL-SERL all require) or a hand-built heuristic. Our tactile ACC/ACE heads already emit exactly the p_contact/CoP/wrench signal a reward classifier would otherwise have to be trained separately to produce — that's a legitimate, citable "our contact anticipation model enables cheap autonomous RL fine-tuning that a vision-only student cannot get for free" angle, which ties the RL result back into the paper's central bet instead of leaving it as an orphan systems fix.

## 5. Risks

- **Mode-selection vs. post-hoc correction**: if slow/fast descent mode is set by the persistent noise draw rather than downstream execution, an action-space residual may not be able to override it — see DSRL-lite fallback above.
- **Reward hacking against your own classifier**: since ACC/ACE are part of the *same* teacher network being probed, an RL policy could learn to game states where ACC over-reports contact rather than truly improving grasp success; sanity-check with occasional real lift/place verification, not just the gate.
- **Non-stationary base policy**: v4→v5 fine-tuning is still active; freeze one checkpoint for the whole RL phase or the residual will chase a moving target.
- **Rig-hour budget is the binding constraint, not sample count**: automate resets aggressively (self-resetting task or a fixed teleop reset script) — ResFiT/ZPRL's wall-clock numbers assume operator-driven resets, which we may not be able to afford at their cadence with only 2 operators.
- **Off-policy hyperparameters under real-time constraints**: REDQ/DDPG-style critics need tuning (UTD ratio, ensemble size); a bad first configuration burns rig-hours you don't have room to redo — pilot the critic/replay-buffer code path on logged v4/v5 rollout data (`planner_trace.json`) before spending live rig time.
- **Bounded residual may be too small to fix a 65–120 mm gap**: if ±15 mm/s velocity bias isn't enough headroom, may need a staged approach (start wide, shrink bound as it converges) rather than a single fixed α.

## 6. Top 3 papers to read in full

1. **ResFiT — 2509.19301** (Residual Off-Policy RL for Finetuning BC Policies): closest mechanism match to our plan (frozen chunk BC + real-world-only per-step residual, sparse reward, small sample budget); read the appendix for exact REDQ/DDPG hyperparameters and reward-labeling protocol to adapt for our automatic tactile reward.
2. **DSRL — 2506.15799** (Steering Your Diffusion Policy with Latent-Space RL): read the algorithm box and the π0 generalist-policy section closely — it's the only one of the ten validating black-box RL on top of a *large pretrained generalist* policy (structurally closest to our 2B-param DiT teacher/student setup) and gives the clearest per-task episode counts.
3. **ZPRL / Beyond Action Residuals — 2605.19919**: the only paper whose frozen base is explicitly a flow-matching chunk policy; even if not run directly (wall-clock too expensive for us), its ablation of latent-residual vs. action-residual exploration smoothness is directly relevant evidence for the design choice in §3, and it's the fallback if the low-dim action residual underperforms.
